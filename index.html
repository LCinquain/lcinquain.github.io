Research Interests

I am broadly interested in multimodal world models and the latent spaces they learn to predict in. The question I most want to study is:

At what level should language, vision and action share a latent space, and when does sharing help rather than interfere?

My working hypothesis is that modalities should share abstract, event-level representations and keep separate latents for detail, which makes a hierarchical world model the natural way to share selectively. Throughout, I want to judge a latent space by what it lets a model predict and plan, not only by how faithfully it reconstructs. My work so far approaches this from three directions:

  • Hierarchy: separating slow, high-level intent from local motion, designed by hand for long-horizon vessel trajectory prediction [SKETCH].
  • Learning signals: which gradients should reach a stored representation, and how to tell their contributions apart [Policy Memory].
  • Latent design for language: what makes a sentence-level latent space easy for a downstream model to predict in, and how to learn one [ongoing].

Linyong Gan*, Zimo Li*, Wenxin Xu*, Xingjian Li*, Jianhua Z. Huang, Enmei Tu, Shuhang Chen

When Does Backpropagating Through Policy Memory Matter? Separating Representation Credit from Closed-Loop Credit

Xingjian Li, Jianhua Z. Huang, Junli Duan

Under review at Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR), 2026

When a policy is trained through a differentiable simulator, gradients reach its memory along two routes: representation credit, which teaches it what to summarize, and closed-loop credit, which flows through physical states that earlier actions produced. A matched-forward protocol removes one route at a time to show when each matters.

Ongoing Research

Elucidating the Design Space of Continuous Diffusion Language Models

A controlled study that changes one design choice of a continuous diffusion language model at a time under a matched-step protocol, aimed at a stronger base model.

A Learnable Sentence-Level Latent Space for Language

A sentence- and paragraph-level encoder paired with a continuous diffusion decoder, as a learnable alternative to the frozen SONAR space that Large Concept Models predict in. The questions it asks: what properties make a latent space easy for a downstream model to learn to predict in, and how can we obtain a latent space that has them?
